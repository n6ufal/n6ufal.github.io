People often confuse understanding with agreement.

Someone explains why a harmful ideology appeals to people. They break down the psychology, the grievances, the internal logic. Then someone else responds: *so you’re defending it?*

No. Explaining something is not the same as supporting it.

But many people treat deep understanding as suspicious. If you can describe the other side clearly, they assume you sympathize with it. So instead of trying to fully understand things, people simplify them into cartoons. That feels safer socially, but it also makes real analysis impossible.

Part of the problem is emotional. Condemnation feels clear and satisfying. Complexity feels uncertain. People mistake that feeling of certainty for actual understanding.

But outrage alone is not comprehension. A person loudly condemning something may still understand very little about it, because they are reacting to it rather than examining it.

The opposite mistake also happens. Sometimes people think that if they deeply understand something terrible, they now owe it sympathy or leniency. But understanding and judgment are separate things.

A forensic psychologist might understand a serial killer better than anyone else. That does not mean the killer deserves approval or forgiveness. The understanding exists to explain, not excuse.

You can fully understand something and still reject it completely. You can condemn something while still admitting your understanding of it is incomplete.

The only useless approach is refusing to look closely at all.

Real intellectual honesty means being willing to understand things you wish didn’t make sense. It means following the logic wherever it goes without pretending complexity itself is endorsement.

You don’t have to abandon your convictions to do that. You just have to see clearly before judging.
