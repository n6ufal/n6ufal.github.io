There is no shittier feeling than that nagging voice in your head telling you that you are just not enough. You are out here trying your best to keep your head above water and dealing with a mess you didn’t even start, but then someone looks at you and treats you like you are just one big failure. They take everything you are and squash it down into a single moment where you didn’t give them exactly what they wanted.

The worst part is that you start doing their job for them. You start wondering if maybe they actually have a point. You play the whole conversation back on a loop and pick it apart looking for receipts of your own guilt. You ask yourself if you were actually being a dick or if you just totally misread the vibe. That is what happens when someone else's twisted view of you starts living rent-free in your head. You start looking at yourself through their broken lens, and suddenly you can't even remember who you were before they showed up.

Psychologists call this "Splitting." It is basically a defense mechanism where someone cannot wrap their head around the fact that people are complicated. To them, you aren't a real person with a lot on your plate. You are just a character in a script. You are the hero when you say yes, and you are the villain the second you don’t. There is no middle ground and no room for the fact that you might have just had a hellish week.

When someone refuses to see the full picture of who you are, they aren't actually looking at you. They are looking at a projection of whatever they need from you in that moment. I keep coming back to that because the whole spiral of gaslighting yourself happens because some part of you still thinks that if you just explain it the right way, they will finally get it. But the truth is that you have been cast in a role in their head, and no amount of proof is going to change a casting decision they already made.

This is the Fundamental Attribution Error in action. They don’t see that you said no because you are burnt out or overwhelmed. They just see a guy who said no because he’s lazy or because he doesn't give a shit. Your actual life is invisible to them. All they see is the behavior, and they twist it to fit whatever story makes them the victim.

---

It gets even uglier if you are dealing with a narcissist. To them, a boundary feels like a personal attack. Their whole ego is built on a shaky foundation where everyone around them has to be a "yes" man. Your "no" wrecks their whole world. That is not your problem to fix, even though they will definitely try to make you feel like it is.

What I wish someone had told me sooner is that the people who need to hear your boundaries the most are the exact same people who will try to punish you for having them.

We have been trained for years to think that a "no" needs a whole legal defense behind it. We feel like our limits don’t count unless we can back them up with a mountain of evidence. You feel like you owe people an explanation deep enough to survive a courtroom, but that is just you auditioning for the right to have basic needs.

Most of the time, that over-explaining comes from a real place. If you grew up in a house where having boundaries meant a huge fight or someone being cold to you, then saying no feels like pulling the pin on a grenade. So you soften the blow. You hedge your bets. You bury the "no" under so many apologies and so much context that it doesn't even sound like a refusal anymore. Then you are shocked when they walk right over you like you never said anything at all.

There wasn't a boundary there. At least, not a version they could actually feel.

Setting a boundary is actually kind of boring because of how simple it is. You say it once, clearly. You don’t debate it. You don’t re-explain it every time they push back. If you do that, you are just teaching them that if they keep annoying you, you will eventually cave.

The hardest part is never the first time you say it. It is sticking to your guns afterward when they start with the guilt trips and the silent treatment. They will tell you that you’ve changed, that you’re being selfish, or that they don’t even know who you are anymore. The self-doubt will hit you like a truck. You will wonder if you are being too harsh or if one more talk will finally make it click for them.

They won't get it. You cannot explain yourself into being understood by someone who has already decided what your "no" says about them. The only people who get pissed off when you set boundaries are the ones who were winning because you didn’t have any.
