Seen it All Before

May 13, 2026

The more I come to know, the heavier the unknown becomes. Not a wound, exactly, but a hollowing. A slow widening. The horizon is always retreating. You walk toward it for years, decades, and it never draws nearer. Only sharper, only more cruelly out of reach.

The one who only skimmed the surface walked away satisfied. I stayed. And staying cost me that small, bright peace. I know now where the ground grows soft. Where the clean answers crumble. Where a quiet tug on the wrong thread will undo everything you thought was certain. So I hesitate where they did not. I hedge where they spoke plainly. And somewhere beneath the years, that began to look like doubt from the outside. When all it ever was, was the slow bruise of honesty.

Socrates sat in that ache. Refused to pretend he knew what he hadn’t examined, and chose to live inside that confession rather than smooth it over. There is something almost grief-stricken in that figure. A man who looked so long, so clearly, that the looking itself became the only thing left to hold. The Stoics felt it differently. Hold everything loosely, they said. Not because you are small, but because every certainty is dying, even now. The Zen teachers named it beginner’s mind and called it freedom. But I think for most of us it feels more like a door closing. You cannot go back to the bright, thoughtless faith of not yet knowing what you know.

The scars are quieter than any philosophy admits. They are simply: I was sure once. I was wrong. And it mattered. Then again. Then again. After enough of that, certainty stops feeling like strength and starts feeling like something you can no longer afford. You still move. You still decide, build, commit. But you hold everything more loosely now. With the low ache of someone who remembers the ground giving way.

The ones who never stayed long enough to be broken are still certain. Still loud. Still filling rooms with the sharp, unbothered confidence of people who left just before the complexity arrived.

I don’t exactly envy them. But some nights, some cold quiet nights, I almost do.
