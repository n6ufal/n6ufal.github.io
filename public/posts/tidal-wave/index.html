Somatic

May 13, 2026

I watched it happen from the ceiling, or some nameless place beyond it, I stared down at a frame I used to call mine. Utterly frozen, like a rabbit beneath a hawk’s shadow.

No one warns you that freezing feels like getting evicted from yourself. That the self just flees to a safe distance while the rest of you stays behind to handle the damage. I saw everything from up there. Every detail, sharp as a razor, the kind of clarity you spend years trying to drown or drink or sleep into oblivion. Then I came back down and started the lie: That didn’t happen. Over and over until it almost sounded true. That didn’t happen. Go to sleep. You’re fine.

But I couldn’t sweet-talk my way unconscious. Some part of me knew the truth, and it wouldn’t shut up.

My body—and I have never fully forgiven it—my body said okay. Not me. I never said okay. But the body has its own humiliating, senseless logic, and it responded. And that response became the thing I locked in a room, the thing I couldn’t look at, the thing that made me want to claw out of my own skin. How do you live inside something that betrayed you like that? How do you wash it, dress it, drag it around, pretend it’s yours?

You don’t.

You just start hating it quietly. You learn to be a stranger in your own flesh and call that surviving.

Suppression from the inside looks like nothing at all. It looks like being funny, being fine, being the one who holds everything together. You build a tidy little room inside your head, lock something horrible in there, stand in front of the door smiling, and say, What door? The lying was just the next breath, and the next. The world kept asking me to show up, and I kept showing up. Beneath all that showing up, something was rotting.

The why, why, why came later. Oh, they always do.

Someone brushes your shoulder by accident, and your whole nervous system files it as evidence. Why did I freeze? Why didn’t I fight? Why did my body—why did I let— And that’s where the floor caves in. That’s where blame finds the fastest route home: inward, always inward. As if the part of me that fled to the ceiling did something wrong by leaving. As if I should have stayed and fought from inside a body that had already decided the safest thing was not to be there at all.

Eventually, the suppression became the entire house I lived in. I built a whole version of myself that it had never happened to. That version was functional. Even likable. Just hollow, exhausting to operate, and quietly falling apart at the seams.

Then it all came out like water finding every crack at once. Slow, then everywhere. The room I had sealed and painted over stood wide open, and everything I had locked inside was still wet, still loud, still exactly where I left it. Still mine.

I still see that part of me that went to the ceiling. For a long time, I hated it for leaving. But I think it went up there because it didn’t know what else to do. I think it’s been waiting for me to stop punishing it. To understand that it left because staying would have broken something irreparable.

I couldn’t sing myself to sleep.

But I’m still here. Skin and all.
