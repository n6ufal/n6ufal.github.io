Do you remember your lucky number?

May 2, 2026 · 3 min

I was never the kind of person who got nervous around people my age. That just wasn’t my thing. But then there was this one girl in class I couldn’t figure out how to talk to, so naturally, I just… didn’t.

And somehow, despite my complete lack of game, she was the one who started things. Which I then managed to completely fumble by ghosting her out of anxiety. Looking back, that might actually be one of the dumbest, most self-sabotaging things I’ve ever done.

That was seven years ago.

Seven years sounds like it should feel heavy, like something you can hold in your hands and measure. But it doesn’t. It just moves. We started at the tail end of high school and came out the other side with degrees — and everything that sentence quietly skips over is the whole story.

I read the other day that your brain doesn’t really finish developing until around twenty-five. The parts responsible for emotional regulation, decision-making, handling conflict — still being built well into your twenties.

So when we met, we weren’t finished people. I was basically a work in progress with questionable judgment and zero impulse control. And a lot of who I am now took shape during those years, while you were there watching it happen in real time and, for some reason, putting up with it.

At some point along the way, you basically became a second mother figure to me.

Most people go through that kind of becoming alone, or around people who don’t stick around. We didn’t. We went through it side by side, which in hindsight feels a little miraculous given the version of me you had to deal with. And somewhere in all of that, without even trying, you made me believe in miracles.

The strange thing about seven years is that you can’t actually feel its size. Memory doesn’t store time in a straight line — it keeps the peaks, the breaks, the moments with some kind of edge. Everything routine gets compressed. There’s a name for it: the holiday paradox. Familiar days barely leave a trace because the brain stops recording what it already knows. Seven years of ordinary Tuesdays don’t take up much space. A single bad week can feel larger than an entire good year.

Which means most of what those days actually felt like is gone now, folded down into a vague sense of before and after. That doesn’t feel sad to me. If anything, it might be the closest thing to proof that we became comfortable enough that time stopped needing to announce itself.

Also, speaking of seven — there’s Se7en (1995), directed by David Fincher. I’m thinking of rewatching it just to lean into our dumb little number superstition. And apparently the number 27 shows up a lot in Shed (2011) by Title Fight.

2,557 days. 84 months.

I don’t really have the words for what seven years with you means. I don’t think anyone does. But every version of me that exists now — the one who learned how to be a person, who made it through college, who slowly figured out what he wants — exists because you were there for it.

So happy birthday, Safa. And happy anniversary to us.

Here’s to everything we’ve already survived, and everything still ahead. Also, Adi Brata Broto sends his regards from the heavens above.

And just to remind you how little I sometimes understand you: you once got food poisoning from sushi on a holiday trip, and today we’re celebrating by eating sushi. My caveman brain still hasn’t processed that logic. Then again, you probably feel the same way about me half the time. Which, I guess, is part of how this has worked for this long.

I love you.
